Why do India's scorching days give way to drenching monsoon rains?

The summer monsoon begins with a seasonal shift in the winds.

As land heats much faster than the ocean, pressure falls over land.

Moist winds carry ocean water inland from the Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal.

A northward moving tropical rain belt helps organize this vast seasonal circulation.

Rising moist air cools, condensing its moisture into clouds and eventually rain.

The Western Ghats lift these moist winds, bringing heavy rain to their windward slopes.

Terrain shapes where rain falls; wet spells and breaks shape its rhythm.

From June to September, these rains sustain crops and replenish water supplies.

Understanding the monsoon means following heat, winds, moisture, and the seasonal rhythm connecting them.
